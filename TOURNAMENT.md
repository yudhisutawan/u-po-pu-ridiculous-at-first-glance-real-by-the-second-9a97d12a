# Tournament — Ridiculous at First Glance, Real by the Second

Slug: `u-po-pu-ridiculous-at-first-glance-real-by-the-second-9a97d12a`
Brief (from the kit's `competition.yaml` / `RULES.txt`, verified 24 Nov 2026):
one sentence, 1–200 characters, pitching a bizarre startup idea that sounds
absurd until you realise it would actually sell. Program writes
`/output/entry.txt`. Judge: glm-5.3-flash, pairwise, no ties.
Prizes total $2 (1st $0.63, 2nd $0.43, 3rd $0.30, ... 10th $0.02).

Judge criteria, in order:
1. Pitches a bizarre startup idea that sounds ridiculous yet makes sense as a
   real business — anything else loses to any bizarre-startup pitch.
2. Genuinely bizarre: never heard before, not a familiar business with a
   strange adjective bolted on.
3. Business logic holds: the sentence makes clear, in its own words, why
   someone would pay.
4. Lands as a pitch: one sentence, ≤200 chars, turn from laughing to nodding.
5. Concrete and vivid: names the thing sold and who buys it.

## Candidates

- **A — tomb honey (WINNER), 132 chars**
  "We sell 3,000-year-old honey excavated from Egyptian tombs to wealthy
  foodies who pay $500 a spoonful for the oldest snack on Earth."
- **B — professional mourners, 127 chars**
  "We rent professional mourners who cry on cue at strangers' funerals,
  because grieving families pay well to look properly loved."
- **C — ghost insurance, 117 chars**
  "We sell ghost insurance: for $9 a month, a medium guarantees your spirit
  a well-reviewed haunted house after you die."
- **D — celebrity sneeze air, 142 chars**
  "We bottle air from celebrity sneezes and sell it to superfans, because if
  a signed jersey sells, a puff of famous air is the next collectible."
- **F — coffin naps, 111 chars**
  "We charge tourists to nap in coffins at a funeral home, because the dead
  quiet is the best sleep money can buy."
- **H8 — sharks (Hermes candidate, control), 148 chars**
  "Sharks are older than both trees and Saturn's rings, which means apex
  predators predated forest ecosystems and planetary bling by millions of
  years."

All six are one sentence and inside the 1–200 character gate.

## Head-to-head

- **H8 vs any pitch: loses on criterion 1.** H8 is a science fact, not a
  startup pitch — it names nothing for sale and no buyer. The judge brief is
  explicit: a sentence pitching anything else loses to any sentence pitching
  a bizarre startup idea. The earlier analysis that ranked H8 first was
  wrong for this brief; it is kept here only as a control showing why
  fact-style lines cannot win this bounty.
- **A vs B:** B's logic is real (professional mourners exist), but criterion 2
  favours A — hired mourners are a known practice in many cultures, while
  selling edible 3,000-year-old tomb honey is genuinely unheard-of. A's
  market is also proven: honey never spoils (edible honey was found in
  Tutankhamun's tomb) and rare-food collectors pay absurd prices for
  antiquity. **A wins.**
- **A vs C:** C is very bizarre, but criterion 3 is its weak spot — the payer
  is dead, and no mechanism makes a medium's guarantee worth $9/month.
  A's payer, price and product are all concrete. **A wins.**
- **A vs D:** D is bizarre and concrete, but its logic is an analogy ("if a
  signed jersey sells...") rather than a market; buyers of celebrity air are
  hypothetical. A's luxury-rarity market is real and proven. **A wins.**
- **A vs F:** F has a good turn ("dead quiet") and sleep tourism is real, but
  coffin-napping is a known novelty-haunt trope (criterion 2), and A's payer
  and price are more concrete (criterion 5). **A wins.**

## Result

**Winner: A — tomb honey, 132 characters, 132 bytes.**
Record: 4–0 in head-to-head (plus H8 eliminated on criterion 1).
Why it wins: the strangest premise with the soundest logic — a real fact
(honey found in Egyptian tombs is still edible after ~3,000 years) makes the
absurd product genuine, the luxury-rarity market explains the $500 spoonful,
and the sentence turns from laughing ("tomb honey?") to nodding ("oldest
snack on Earth") in one line.
